# Food profile

## Strong recurring signals

Analysis of 17 REWE eBons covered 386 food line items and 98 consolidated foods. The most frequent items were:

- Skyr.
- Broccoli.
- Kiwi.
- Eggs.
- Mini cucumbers.
- Cherry tomatoes.
- Natural mixed nuts.
- Blueberries.

A later large Flink order reinforced these categories and added strong signals for frozen vegetables, chicken, fish, legumes, mushrooms, kimchi, fruit, oats, yogurt, kefir, rice cakes, oils, and spices.

## Confirmed or explicitly stated rhythms

- Skyr or similar thick natural yogurt such as Fage: one 500 g unit per day in rotation, not both on the same day. During the current work routine, approximately two of fourteen two-week portions are bought in Munich on weekly commute days, so the home-supplied two-week target is about twelve units. Check printed best-before dates.
- Oats: approximately 50–60 g per day. Three 500 g packs cover about 25–30 days and form the current four-week replenishment quantity.
- Natural nuts: approximately 50 g per day.
- Ground coffee: Melitta Auslese Klassisch, approximately 15 g per day. The prior roughly 30–35 g/day amount was reduced because it was too strong.
- Protein UHT milk: two 1 L cartons per week.
- Fish tins: approximately two to three per week, with a preference for mackerel and sardines as convenient omega-3 sources; tuna in its own juice is also used.
- Frozen chicken, suitable salmon, and approved prawn formats share one monthly freezer target of approximately 6 kg, based on 200 g/day for 30 days. Current chicken stock is 800 g. Fish tins are a separate pantry reserve and do not automatically reduce this freezer target because they may be eaten as an additional portion.
- Rice cakes: roughly one to two packs per week.
- Mushrooms such as chanterelles: approximately one pack every one to two weeks.

Quantities describe prior planning and should be reconciled with current inventory before large purchases.

## Protein and dairy

- Skyr is a frequent staple.
- Magerquark and Skyr are mutually exclusive alternatives, not additive portions. Arthur currently prefers Skyr and has removed Magerquark from the recurring rotation because of its salt content; do not use quark as an automatic Skyr substitute.
- Fage Total yogurt 0.2%, kefir, quark, protein UHT milk, eggs, chicken, salmon, canned fish, and legumes have all been used.
- A large Flink order contained seven 400 g packs of pre-cooked frozen `ja!` chicken fillet steaks, making this a strong batch-cooking signal.
- Eggs are reviewed in the fourteen-day cycle, not weekly. Historical fresh-egg consumption was about four/day, or approximately five 10-packs per two weeks, and five packs fit in the refrigerator. The current fresh-egg order is only a one-pack Flink delivery experiment because transport breakage is uncertain. Future two-week quantities must be decided jointly with the remaining freeze-dried egg stock. Current freeze-dried stock is 1 kg; at roughly 50 g/day it represents about 20 use days, but a possible digestive or general well-being issue is only an observation, not an established intolerance or causal relationship.

## Carbohydrate and legume rotation

- Prefer rotation rather than relying heavily on rice.
- Bread, including pumpernickel and packaged whole-grain bread, is currently paused because Arthur wants to remain bread-free. Retain it as a possible future option, but do not surface or substitute it for another carbohydrate unless he explicitly reactivates it.
- Frequently discussed options: brown or green lentils, black beans, white beans, black/Beluga lentils, buckwheat flakes, and a small amount of chickpeas. Microwave rice is currently paused because ready-to-eat lentils, beans, and chickpeas fully replace it. Ready-to-eat cooked potatoes and buckwheat flakes are also paused because Flink does not offer suitable delivered products. Retain all three for possible future reactivation rather than deleting them.
- Beans and lentils are preferred over chickpeas.
- Ready-to-eat legumes are a monthly pantry purchase. Current consumption is approximately four to five tins, jars, or pouches per week; the working target is about 20 units after replenishment, with beans and lentils favored over chickpeas.
- Dry grains that require stovetop cooking are poor defaults; ready-to-eat tins, jars, pouches, or flakes are better fits.

## Fruit, vegetables, and fermented foods

- Vegetable target: approximately 300 g per day across broccoli, cauliflower, spinach, Brussels sprouts, kale, and similar vegetables rather than 300 g of each item.
- Fresh broccoli: at most one head per weekly purchase because a second fresh head may spoil before use. Additional coverage should come from frozen vegetables.
- Fresh carrots: approximately 1 kg per week.
- Recurrent or observed foods include broccoli, cauliflower, spinach, jarred mushrooms, red pepper, cooked vacuum-packed beetroot, cucumbers, tomatoes, blueberries, strawberries, kiwi, grapes, bananas, apples, lemons, avocado, pomegranate seeds, and melon. Arthur's usual beetroot is not fresh raw beetroot.
- Kimchi and sauerkraut fit the established pattern; both original and mild kimchi have been purchased.
- Sauerkraut is currently paused. It was originally chosen for expected live cultures, but ordinary supermarket products did not reliably meet that purpose; Kimchi now fully replaces it. Retain sauerkraut for possible future reactivation, but do not treat the two as additive fermented-food staples.
- Complete Organics kimchi in 240 g refrigerated jars: current working consumption is approximately two jars per week.

## Preference and friction rules

- Organic certification is not a sufficient benefit by itself to justify a large price premium.
- Price, availability, convenience, and consistency matter strongly.
- Arthur wants food in usable condition and does not consider small refunds a good substitute for receiving the required food.
- Historically, fruit, berries, grapes, broccoli, eggs, and raw meat or fish were treated as quality-sensitive because selection and transport condition matter. This is a preference signal, not an absolute ban on delivery. Do not include Arthur's normal jarred mushrooms in this fresh-food category.
- Dark chocolate is paused in Arthur's diet and recurring shopping. Retain it for possible future reactivation, but do not surface or buy it unless he explicitly reactivates it; this replaces the earlier one-pack maximum.
- Fresh apples are currently paused after a systematic local quality problem: Arthur inspected multiple REWE crates and varieties, observed recurring discoloration or texture changes around the stem area that predicted internal spoilage, and still discarded more than half of the least-bad selected apples; an office apple also had internal pest damage. Do not add apples to a cart unless Arthur explicitly restarts them after local supply quality changes.
- Fruit consumption works as a shared volume across interchangeable fruits. Removing apples increases the required amount of bananas, kiwi, grapes, berries, or other accepted fruit rather than simply reducing total fruit intake.
- Bananas: current working rhythm is approximately one per day, purchased weekly rather than as a two-week fresh supply.
- Fresh blueberries: when selected, current consumption is approximately 100 g per day. Blueberries have not been the berry that spoiled quickly in Arthur's recent experience; that correction applies to raspberries.
- Fresh raspberries: have spoiled quickly in Arthur's experience, so treat them as a short-lived optional item rather than stocking them for many days.
- Fresh berries have one shared weekly cap: all fresh blueberry, raspberry, strawberry, and other berry varieties combined must total no more than 500 g. Any additional berry supply should be frozen rather than fresh.
- Fresh whole pomegranate creates too much preparation friction and may spoil, so it is not a default. Prefer frozen ready-to-use pomegranate seeds when the freezer routine is working.
- Watermelon is the accepted fresh melon: buy one small whole fruit or a small pre-cut segment weekly, never a large volume that one person cannot finish promptly. Honeydew or similar yellow melon is paused after a spoiled interior. Whole and pre-cut watermelon are separate storage forms; never plan a pre-cut segment as two-week stock.
- Avoid frozen-fruit workflows that require opening the freezer, weighing a daily portion, reclosing the bag, and returning it every evening; this repeated handling has reliably caused skipped consumption. Prefer factory-separated inner sachets or a one-time division of a new bag into closed one-to-two-day freezer portions. A fully thawed 500 g berry mix is not a seven-day refrigerator solution.
- Frozen raspberries, blueberries, strawberries, and pomegranate seeds are freezer bulk-stock items. Replenish them together during a periodic freezer order rather than treating them as weekly fresh purchases; thaw only enough for approximately two to three days at a time.
- Monthly frozen-fruit baseline: approximately 100 g/day rotating among strawberries, raspberries, and pomegranate seeds. A convenient current target is two 500 g strawberry packs, two 500 g raspberry packs, and four 300 g pomegranate packs, totaling 3.2 kg or about 32 days. Separately keep one 500 g frozen-blueberry pack as a five-day backup for failed, missed, or poor-quality fresh-blueberry deliveries. The backup is not part of the ordinary red-fruit rotation.
